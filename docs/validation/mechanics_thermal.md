## 2. Long thin solenoid: hoop stress from the Lorentz body force

Winding inner radius 0.5 m, height 10.0 m, 1e+06 ampere-turns, E = 100 GPa, nu = 0.3, no supports.
The magnetic pressure p = (B_in^2 - B_out^2)/2 mu0 is taken from the solved field just inside and just outside the winding at mid-height, so the comparison tests the mechanics only. A thin shell carries the exact thickness-mean hoop stress for this load (**not** a membrane approximation): radial equilibrium with f_r = J B_z(r) and the linear profile B_z = B0 (b - r)/t integrates to (1/t) int sigma_tt dr = p (Rm - t/6)/t. The membrane value p Rm/t is kept beside it as the leading-order comparison. The two halves attract with p pi R^2, giving sigma_zz = -p R^2/(2 Rm t) at mid-height -- that one IS a membrane value, and its O(t/R) error is what the last column shows (it halves as t halves).

The radial resultant of the body force integrated by the elasticity assembly agrees with the independent winding force of `results["forces"]` to 2.2e-16 (same quadrature, different code path).

The sigma_zz residual is a thickness effect, not an end effect: at t/R = 0.02 it is 1.79e-2 for H/R = 20, 40 and 80 alike, while it halves with t at fixed H/R. It is not replaced by the exact axial-resultant identity int sigma_zz dA = -F_z, because an area-weighted integral of the recovered sigma_zz across the full wall is dominated by the stress recovery at the two loaded surfaces (several per cent), which is the quantity this table deliberately excludes elsewhere.

The toroid's sheath carries essentially no magnetic load: a toroidal field is confined inside the winding, so the gap between winding and sheath is field free. Its displacement is the cooldown contraction. The solenoid template's coax walls are copper, i.e. transparent to the static field, so they too see only the cooldown.

**The CORE row is a warning, not a result.** At the template's default 1e6 ampere-turns the cavity surface field is 0.516 T, far above niobium's critical field (the template's own `cost.sc_wall_cap` is 0.086 T), so the magnetic pressure B^2/2 mu0 is about 0.1 MPa. The cavity's flat end walls are 1 cm thick and span 1.4 m, and linear thin-plate bending under 0.1 MPa gives exactly what the table shows: ~0.18 m of deflection and ~300 MPa of bending stress. A deflection of 18 times the wall thickness is far outside small-deflection elasticity, and buckling is not modelled, so the number only says that this cavity needs a structural design (ribs, supports, thicker walls) at this excitation. It does illustrate that the solver picks up plate bending, not only membrane stress.

## 7. Conduction with k(T): the analytic Kirchhoff solution

The same geometry with a two-point table k(T) = 10 (1 + 0.08 (T - 4)) W/(m K) over [4, 34] K, i.e. k rises by a factor 3.4 over the table. The Kirchhoff potential theta = int k dT satisfies the linear Poisson equation, so theta = q (R^2 - r^2)/4 and T = T0 + (sqrt(1 + 2 c theta/k0) - 1)/c exactly. The solver uses a Picard iteration on k(T_prev) instead, so the table is a genuine test of the nonlinear path. Temperature rise 13.1174 K.

## 8. Conduction between two prescribed temperatures (the cold-stage heat load)

A wall r = 0.4..0.5 m, height 1.0 m, k = 10 W/(m K), carried between a sink at 4.0 K and one at 300.0 K. Exact: Q = 2 pi k H dT / ln(b/a) = 83346.475395 W and T = T_cold + dT ln(r/a)/ln(b/a).

`outflow_W` is the **net** reaction over all prescribed-temperature degrees of freedom, so here it cancels to round-off: with heat entering at one boundary and leaving at another it is not the heat load. `heat_removed_W` (the sinks that absorb heat) and `heat_added_W` (the boundaries that supply it) are reported separately, and `balance_relative` is scaled by the largest of generated / removed / added, so it is meaningful even with no internal generation.
