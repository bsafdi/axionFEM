($\Psi$, $L_p$, and optionally losses/$Q$): unit conventions, axion inputs,
the readout-circuit signal and noise PSDs, Asimov statistics, the optimal scan
rate, the scan requirement $\mathcal F_{\rm req}$, time allocation, reach curves,

## 1. Units and conversions

### 1.1 Natural Heaviside–Lorentz (HL) units

axionFEM reports natural HL values next to SI ones. Natural HL units: $\hbar=c=k_B=\varepsilon_0=\mu_0=1$,
$\alpha=e^2/4\pi$, so $\nabla\times\mathbf B=\mathbf J$ and the field energy
density is $B^2/2$. Energies are in eV. Magnetic fields are *quoted* in tesla
and lengths (including inductances) in metres; the conversions are exact
consequences of the SI definitions (CODATA 2018; $\mu_0$ is the CODATA-2018
value $1.25663706212\times10^{-6}$ H/m, which differs from $4\pi\times10^{-7}$ by
$5.5\times10^{-10}$):

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| $\hbar c$ | $1.973269804\times10^{-7}$ eV m |
| $\hbar$ | $6.582119569\times10^{-16}$ eV s |
| 1 m | $5.067730716\times10^{6}$ eV$^{-1}$ |
| 1 s | $1.519267447\times10^{15}$ eV$^{-1}$ |
| 1 K | $8.617333262\times10^{-5}$ eV |
| 1 T | $\sqrt{(\hbar c)^3/(\mu_0 e)} = 195.352771$ eV$^2$ (HL; numerically with $\hbar c$ in eV m, $\mu_0$ in H/m, $e$ in J/eV; from $B_{\rm HL}^2/2=B_{\rm SI}^2/2\mu_0$) |
| 1 Wb = 1 T m² | $5.017029284\times10^{15}$ (dimensionless) |
| 1 GeV/cm³ | $7.683505562\times10^{-6}$ eV$^4$ |
| $e_{\rm HL}=\sqrt{4\pi\alpha}$ | 0.3028221209; check: $\Phi_0=\pi/e_{\rm HL}=10.37438$ = $h/2e$ in Wb × 5.01703e15 |
| 1 yr | Julian, $3.15576\times10^{7}$ s |

### 1.2 FEM-facing geometric quantities

The FEM supplies two numbers per mode, defined in natural HL with the current
normalised out (so they are pure geometry × field):

$$\Psi \equiv \frac1I\int d^3x\,\mathbf A_I\cdot\mathbf B_{\rm lab}\quad[\mathrm{T\,m^3}],\qquad
L_p\equiv\frac1{I^2}\int d^3x\,|\mathbf B_I|^2=\frac1{I^2}\int d^3x\,\mathbf J_I\cdot\mathbf A_I\quad[\mathrm m] \tag{S1.1–S1.2}$$

where $\mathbf A_I$ solves $\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf A_I=\mathbf J_I$ ($\mu_0\to1$), so
$\mathbf A_I/I$ is dimensionless and $\mathbf B_I/I$ has units m$^{-1}$.
Equivalently: solve in SI and divide $\Psi$ and $L$ by $\mu_0$.
Sanity check (long coaxial solenoid): $\Psi=HR^2B(1-x^2)/4$, $L_p=(H/2\pi)\ln(1/x)$.

SI and eV forms:

$$\Psi_{\rm SI}=\mu_0\Psi\ [\mathrm{T\,H\,m^2}],\qquad L_{\rm SI}=\mu_0L_p\ [\mathrm H] \tag{S1.3}$$
$$\mathcal F\equiv\Psi^4/L_p^2\ [\mathrm{T^4m^{10}}],\qquad \mathcal F_{\rm SI}=\mu_0^2\,\mathcal F\ [\mathrm{T^4H^2m^8}],\qquad 1\,\mathrm{T^4m^{10}}=1.627097598\times10^{76}\ \mathrm{eV^{-2}} \tag{S1.4}$$

with $1\,\mathrm{T\,m^3}=2.542495342\times10^{22}$ eV$^{-1}$ (for $\Psi$) and
$1\,\mathrm m=5.0677307\times10^{6}$ eV$^{-1}$ (for $L_p$).
Stored energy $W_{\rm mag}=\tfrac12\int B^2d^3x$ evaluated with $B$ in T and lengths in m is in
"T² m³", and

$$1\ \mathrm{T^2m^3}\ (\text{HL})=1/\mu_0\ \mathrm J=0.795774715\ \mathrm{MJ}. \tag{S1.5}$$

### 1.3 Axion coupling constant $\kappa_a$ and flux

$$\kappa_a\equiv g_{a\gamma\gamma}\sqrt{2\rho_{\rm DM}}\ [\mathrm{eV}],\qquad \Phi_a=\kappa_a\Psi \ \ (\text{flux **amplitude**, dimensionless}) \tag{S2.2}$$

SI: $\kappa_a[\mathrm m^{-1}]=\kappa_a[\mathrm{eV}]/\hbar c=g_{\rm SI}\sqrt{2\rho_{\rm SI}\hbar c}$
with $g_{\rm SI}[\mathrm J^{-1}]=g[\mathrm{GeV^{-1}}]/(1.602176634\times10^{-10})$ and $\rho_{\rm SI}$ in J/m³. Then

$$\Phi_a[\mathrm{Wb}]=\kappa_a[\mathrm m^{-1}]\;\Psi[\mathrm{T\,m^3}]=\kappa_a^{\rm SI}\Psi_{\rm SI},\qquad \kappa_a^{\rm SI}=\kappa_a[\mathrm m^{-1}]/\mu_0\ [\mathrm H^{-1}],
\qquad \mathbf J_{\rm eff}^{\rm SI}=\kappa_a[\mathrm m^{-1}]\,\mathbf B_{\rm lab}/\mu_0 \tag{S2.3}$$

Check ($g=10^{-19}$ GeV$^{-1}$, $\rho=0.3$ GeV/cm³): $\kappa_a=2.14711512\times10^{-31}$ eV
$=1.08810013\times10^{-24}$ m$^{-1}$ $=8.65882567\times10^{-19}$ H$^{-1}$.

$E/N$: KSVZ 0, DFSZ-I 8/3, DFSZ-II 2/3. $C_\gamma$ configurable: 1.92(4) (chiral PT, 1511.02867),
**1.77(8) (lattice, Brandt et al., arXiv:2603.29153; axionFEM default)**, 1.965(35) (lattice, Lin et al.,
arXiv:2609.22698).
At $m_a=0.5$ neV ($f=120.8995$ kHz):

|---|---|---|---|
| 1.92 | 1.95606e-19 | 7.60689e-20 | 1.27687e-19 |

(GeV$^{-1}$; $g\propto m_a$.) $\rho_{\rm DM}=0.3$ GeV/cm³ $=2.305051669\times10^{-6}$ eV⁴.

### 2.2 Field model and lab-frame speed distribution

The local field is a sum of many waves with random phases and Rayleigh amplitudes
([F] Eqs. 3–10), $\langle a^2\rangle=\rho/m_a^2$. In the MQS limit
$\mathbf J_{\rm eff}=g\,\partial_ta\,\mathbf B_{\rm lab}$, the pickup flux is
$\Phi(t)=g\Psi\,\partial_ta$ and the EMF is $V(t)=-\dot\Phi$; the amplitude of $\Phi$ is $\kappa_a\Psi$,
of $V$ is $m_a\kappa_a\Psi$.

SHM lab-frame speed distribution ([F] Eq. 9; $v$ in units of $c$; $\int_0^\infty f\,dv=1$; no escape-speed cut):

$$f(v)=\frac{v}{\sqrt\pi\,v_0v_{\rm obs}}\Big[e^{-(v-v_{\rm obs})^2/v_0^2}-e^{-(v+v_{\rm obs})^2/v_0^2}\Big],\qquad v_0=220,\ v_{\rm obs}=232\ \mathrm{km/s}. \tag{S2.4}$$

Normalised line shape in angular frequency, $\omega=m_a(1+v^2/2)$, $d\omega=m_av\,dv$:

$$\mathcal L(\omega)=\frac{f(v)}{m_a v}\Big|_{v=\sqrt{2(\omega/m_a-1)}},\qquad\int_0^\infty\mathcal L\,d\omega=1. \tag{S2.5}$$

### 2.3 Axion quality factor

$$Q_a\equiv\int_0^\infty dv\,\frac{f(v)^2}{v}=m_a\int d\omega\,\mathcal L(\omega)^2 \tag{S2.6}$$

(the line-shape factor in [F] Eq. 44.) Computed values:

| halo | $Q_a$ |
|---|---|
| **SHM $v_0=220$, $v_{\rm obs}=232$ km/s** | **6.779483×10⁵** (the default `Q_axion` is the rounded 6.8×10⁵) |
| $v_0=220$, $v_{\rm obs}=220$ | 7.071018×10⁵ |
| $v_0=220$, $v_{\rm obs}=247$ (≈ June maximum) | 6.435062×10⁵ |
| DMRadio's 6.4×10⁵ ⇔ $v_{\rm obs}=248.585$ km/s at $v_0=220$ | 6.4×10⁵ |
| $v_0=235$, $v_{\rm obs}=250$ | 5.899375×10⁵ |
| [C] App. B literal ($\sigma_v=\sqrt{3/2}\,v_c$ in $e^{-v^2/2\sigma_v^2}$, $v_d=235.2$) | 3.13×10⁵ (≠ [C] B9's 4.4×10⁵; Sec. 9) |

Coherence time (order of magnitude): $\tau_{\rm coh}\sim1/(f\,v_0v_{\rm obs})$ ([F] Sec. III B) $=14.6$ s at 120.9 kHz, or
$\sim Q_a/f=5.6$ s.

## 3. Readout circuit and noise model

### 3.1 Circuit

Series $R$–$L$–$C$ loop: pickup inductance $L=L_p$ (input-coil inductance neglected; otherwise
the rate below is multiplied by $(L_p/L_{\rm tot})^2$, [C] Eq. F37), tunable $C$, series resistance
$R$ lumping all losses, read out by a SQUID through mutual inductance $M$; SQUID input
loading and imprecision–backaction correlations neglected (the readout network of
[readout_circuit.md](readout_circuit.md) removes these simplifications).

$$Z(\omega)=R(\omega)+i\omega L+\frac1{i\omega C},\quad \omega_0=\frac1{\sqrt{LC}},\quad R_0\equiv R(\omega_0)=\frac{\omega_0L}{Q},\quad
|Z|^2=R^2+L^2\frac{(\omega^2-\omega_0^2)^2}{\omega^2}\approx R_0^2(1+x^2),\ x\equiv\frac{2Q(\omega-\omega_0)}{\omega_0};
\qquad n(\omega,T)=\frac1{e^{\omega/T}-1}\ (\text{exact BE}). \tag{S3.1}$$

$Q$ is the intrinsic resonator Q (for a flux-to-voltage amplifier the loaded and internal $Q$ coincide, [C] App. F 3).

### 3.2 PSD normalisation

All PSDs follow [F]: for a stationary $X$,

$$\langle X^2\rangle=\frac1\pi\int_0^\infty d\omega\,S_X(\omega),$$

i.e. $S_X$ is the two-sided PSD per unit $d\nu$ (for a time series, $S_k=(\Delta t)^2|X_k|^2/T$, [F] Eq. 17;
white noise of variance $\sigma^2$ per sample has $S=\sigma^2\Delta t$). It is **half** the
one-sided-in-$\nu$ PSD $S^{(1)}$ used in [C] (Eqs. E2–E8, F2) and [M3] (A10):
$S=S^{(1)}/2$. Ratios $\lambda_a/\lambda_B$ do not depend on the convention; $\eta_A$ and $\xi$ do, and are fixed below.

### 3.3 Signal

$$S_{V,a}(\omega)=\omega^4g^2\Psi^2S_a(\omega),\quad S_a=\frac{\pi\rho}{m_a^2}\mathcal L(\omega)\ \Rightarrow\
S_{V,a}(\omega)=\frac\pi2\,\Phi_a^2\,\frac{\omega^4}{m_a^2}\,\mathcal L(\omega),\qquad \lambda_a(\omega)=\frac{S_{V,a}}{|Z|^2}. \tag{S3.2}$$

Check: $\frac1\pi\int S_{V,a}d\omega=\tfrac12(m_a\Phi_a)^2\langle(\omega/m_a)^4\rangle$ (the $\omega^4/m_a^4$ factor is $1+2.8\times10^{-6}$ for the SHM and may be set to 1).
$\lambda_a$ is the mean PSD of the circuit current; it is exponentially distributed per bin ([F] Eq. 24).

### 3.4 Noise (all referred to the circuit current; natural units, restore $\hbar$ in SI)

$$S_{V,\rm th}(\omega)=\sum_kR_k(\omega)\,\hbar\omega\,[2n(\omega,T_k)+1]\quad(\to2k_BTR\ \text{classically}) \tag{S3.3}$$

(one-sided equivalent $4\hbar\omega R(n+\tfrac12)$, [C] F2, [M3] A10). SQUID imprecision (flux PSD $S_\Phi$) enters as
current noise $S_\Phi/M^2$, flat in $\omega$; backaction (current PSD $S_J$) enters as series voltage $\omega^2M^2S_J$,
filtered like the signal (S3.4). Amplifier noise and coupling parameters:

$$\eta_A\equiv\frac{2\sqrt{S_\Phi S_J}}{\hbar},\qquad \xi\equiv\frac{2R_0S_\Phi}{\hbar\,\omega_0M^2}\ \propto M^{-2};\qquad
\frac{S_\Phi}{M^2}=\frac{\hbar\xi\omega_0}{2R_0},\quad M^2S_J=\frac{\hbar\eta_A^2R_0}{2\xi\omega_0},\quad M=\sqrt{\frac{2R_0S_\Phi}{\hbar\omega_0\xi}} \tag{S3.5}$$

$\eta_A=1$ is the SQL. Identities: $\eta_A=\sqrt{S^{(1)}_{II}S^{(1)}_{VV}}/\hbar\omega$ ([M3] A11)
$=k_BT_N^{\min}/(\hbar\omega/2)$ ([C] F51–F52) $=2k_BT_N/\hbar\omega$ ([GUT] A13);
$\xi=\alpha_1$ of [C] F41 $=\bar\alpha$ of [M3] A13. **Do not confuse with [C]'s $\xi_{FV}=Z_N/R$ (F25), which equals $\eta_A/\xi$.**
With correlations, $\eta_A\to2\sqrt{S_\Phi S_J-({\rm Im}\,S_{\Phi J})^2}/\hbar$ leaves $\bar G$ unchanged and only shifts the band
centre ([C] F44–F57); a real correlation is assumed zero. Note on variables: in the flux / circulating-current variables
of `circuit.Squid` the dc SQUID's (Tesche–Clarke) cross PSD $\langle\Phi_nJ^*\rangle$ is *real*; referred to the input it is the
imaginary current–voltage correlation $S_{IV}=-i\omega\langle\Phi_nJ^*\rangle$ of [DMR-SQUID] Eq. 12, i.e. the band-shifting kind,
and the correlated $\eta_A=2\sqrt{S_\Phi S_J-\langle\Phi_nJ^*\rangle^2}/\hbar$ (`Squid.S_PhiJ`, `axionfem.squid`; `squid_chain.md` Secs. 4–5).
**[DMR-SQUID] (arXiv:2504.20398) PSD normalisation:** its $S_{VV},S_{II},S_{IV}$ (Eqs. 5–12, 17) are one-sided per Hz,
$S^{(1)}=2S$ (Johnson $4k_BTR$), although the text under its Eq. 17 calls them "two-sided"; $\eta$ (its Eq. 28) and
$\epsilon_{\rm uc}$ (Eq. 13) therefore equal $\eta_A$ above and $S^{(1)}_\Phi/2L_{\rm sq}=S_\Phi/L_{\rm sq}$.

Total current-noise PSD, the same referred to a series EMF, and the per-bin signal-to-noise ratio:

$$\lambda_B(\omega)=\frac{S_{V,\rm th}+\omega^2M^2S_J}{|Z|^2}+\frac{S_\Phi}{M^2},\qquad
N_V(\omega)\equiv|Z|^2\lambda_B=S_{V,\rm th}+\omega^2M^2S_J+|Z|^2\frac{S_\Phi}{M^2},\qquad
s(\omega)\equiv\frac{\lambda_a}{\lambda_B}=\frac{S_{V,a}}{N_V}. \tag{S3.6}$$

These are the exact (finite-band) expressions used for bin-by-bin sums: full $|Z(\omega)|^2$,
$n(\omega)$ at the bin frequency, backaction $\propto\omega^2$, imprecision flat, and $R(\omega)$ arbitrary
(the reference code allows $R=R_0(\omega/\omega_0)^p$).
Several loss channels: $R=\sum_kR_k$, $Q^{-1}=\sum_kQ_k^{-1}$; in the local formulas below replace
$2n+1\to\sum_k(Q/Q_k)(2n_k+1)$, i.e. in the classical limit $T/Q\to\sum_kT_k/Q_k$.

### 3.5 Local (near-resonance) form, $\bar G$ and optimal coupling

For $|\omega-\omega_0|\ll\omega_0$ with constant $R$, $n$:

$$N_V\simeq\frac{\hbar\omega_0R_0}{2\xi}\big[A+\xi^2x^2\big],\qquad A\equiv\xi^2+2(2n+1)\xi+\eta_A^2. \tag{S3.7}$$
$$\bar G(\xi)=\frac{\xi}{[\xi^2+2(2n+1)\xi+\eta_A^2]^{3/2}}\qquad([C]\ \text{F50, F53};\ [M3]\ \text{A12}) \tag{S3.8}$$
$$\xi_{\rm opt}=\frac{2\eta_A^2}{2n+1+\sqrt{(2n+1)^2+8\eta_A^2}}\qquad([C]\ \text{F54};\ [M3]\ \text{A13}) \tag{S3.9}$$
$$\bar G_{\rm opt}(n,\eta_A)=\bar G(\xi_{\rm opt}) \tag{S3.10}$$

(derivation: $d\bar G/d\xi=0\Leftrightarrow2\xi^2+(2n+1)\xi-\eta_A^2=0$). Limits:
$n\gg1,\ n\gg\eta_A$: $\xi_{\rm opt}\simeq\eta_A^2/(2n+1)$, $A_{\rm opt}\simeq3\eta_A^2$,

$$\bar G_{\rm opt}\simeq\frac1{3\sqrt3\,\eta_A(2n+1)}\simeq\frac{\hbar\omega_0}{6\sqrt3\,\eta_Ak_BT}; \tag{S3.11}$$

$n\to0,\ \eta_A=1$: $\xi_{\rm opt}=1/2$, $\bar G=4/27$ (exact). (Single-frequency dwell instead maximises
$\xi/A$, i.e. $\xi=\eta_A$, "noise matched", [C] F36.) The single-pole resonator with $\xi_{\rm opt}$ reaches ≈75% of the
Bode–Fano bound on integrated sensitivity ([BF]; [C] F35), which justifies the model.

### 3.6 Sensitivity bandwidth (three definitions; full widths)

$$\Delta\omega_{2\times}=\frac{\omega_0}{Q}\frac{\sqrt A}{\xi},\qquad
\Delta\omega_{\rm th>SQ}=\frac{\omega_0}{Q}\frac{\sqrt{2\xi(2n+1)-\eta_A^2-\xi^2}}{\xi},\qquad
\Delta\omega_{\rm eq}=\frac\pi4\frac{\omega_0}Q\frac{\sqrt A}\xi=\int d\omega_0\frac{\theta(\omega_0)}{\theta_{\max}}. \tag{S3.12}$$

$\Delta\omega_{2\times}$: total noise below twice its minimum ([C] F45–F46).
$\Delta\omega_{\rm th>SQ}$: thermal exceeds total SQUID noise ($\simeq(\omega_0/Q)(2n+1)/\eta_A\simeq2k_BT/(\hbar\eta_AQ)$,
i.e. a factor $\sim2T/\eta_A\omega_0$ above the linewidth).
$\Delta\omega_{2\times}/\Delta\omega_{\rm th>SQ}\to\sqrt3$ at $\xi_{\rm opt}$. At high $T$ all are
**independent of $\omega_0$**: e.g. optimistic benchmark $\Delta f_{2\times}=360.90$ Hz,
$\Delta f_{\rm th>SQ}=208.37$ Hz, $\Delta f_{\rm eq}=283.45$ Hz (vs. linewidth 6.04 mHz at 120.9 kHz).

## 4. Statistics

### 4.1 Likelihood and Asimov test statistics

Each tuning $i$ (dwell $t_i$) yields PSD bins at $\omega_k=2\pi k/t_i$, each exponentially distributed with mean
$\lambda_a+\lambda_B$ ([F] Eqs. 24–29); tunings are independent. With
$\Theta(m,g)=2[\ln\mathcal L(g)-\ln\mathcal L(0)]$, $q$ and TS as in [F] Eqs. 35–39, the Asimov values are

$$\Theta(m,g)=\sum_i\sum_kh\big(s_i(\omega_k)\big)\ \xrightarrow{t_i\gg\tau_{\rm coh}}\ \sum_i\frac{t_i}{2\pi}\int_0^\infty d\omega\,h\big(s_i(\omega)\big) \tag{S4.1}$$

with
$$h_{\rm weak}(s)=s^2, \tag{S4.2}$$
$$h_{\rm lim}(s)=2\Big[\ln(1+s)-\frac s{1+s}\Big]=s^2-\tfrac43s^3+\dots\quad(\text{expected limit: null Asimov data, }-q), \tag{S4.3}$$
$$h_{\rm disc}(s)=2\big[s-\ln(1+s)\big]=s^2-\tfrac23s^3+\dots\quad(\text{discovery: signal Asimov data}). \tag{S4.4}$$

(From [F] Eq. 41; the weak form is [F] Eq. 44.) Θ in the weak limit equals the squared
optimal-filter SNR of [C] (F10) and [M3] (Eq. 4); DMRadio's "SNR = 3" means $\Theta=9$.

### 4.2 Limits and discovery

Median expected 95% one-sided limit: $\Theta_{\rm lim}(m,g_{95})=\Theta_{95}=2.71$ ([F] Eq. 54; exact
$[\Phi^{-1}(0.95)]^2=2.705543$). Bands: $|q|=(\Phi^{-1}(0.95)\pm N)^2$: $\pm1\sigma$: 6.99525 / 0.415836;
$\pm2\sigma$: 13.2850 / 0.126129 ([F] Eq. 56; power-constrained at $-1\sigma$) (S4.5).

Look-elsewhere ([F] Eqs. 58–62), $\alpha_{\rm LEE}=3/4$:

$$N_{m_a}=\frac{\ln(f_{\max}/f_{\min})}{\alpha_{\rm LEE}v_0v_{\rm obs}}, \tag{S4.6}$$
$$\mathrm{TS}_{\rm thresh}=\big[\Phi^{-1}(1-p/N_{m_a})\big]^2,\quad p=1-\Phi(\sigma_{\rm global})\ \text{(one-sided, default)}. \tag{S4.7}$$

A scan over 0.5–4.1 neV (120.90–991.38 kHz): $N_{m_a}=4.9402\times10^6$; TS$_{\rm thresh}$ = 38.504 (3σ), 55.073 (5σ)
[two-sided: 37.151, 53.713]. In the weak limit $g_{\rm disc}/g_{95}=(\mathrm{TS}_{\rm thresh}/2.71)^{1/4}=1.9415$ (3σ), 2.1232 (5σ).

## 5. Scan strategy

### 5.1 Local scan rate

Dense scan with dwell density $\tau(\omega_0)$ (time per unit $\omega_0$). For an axion at $m$:
$\Theta(m)=\int d\omega_0\,\tau(\omega_0)\,\frac1{2\pi}\int d\omega\,s^2$. With (S3.2), (S3.7):
$s=S_{V,a}\,2\xi/[\omega_0R_0(A+\xi^2x^2)]$. Doing the $\omega_0$ integral first (valid whenever
$\tau,R,\xi,A$ vary slowly over the sensitivity bandwidth — no narrow-line assumption needed),
$\int d\omega_0(A+\xi^2x^2)^{-2}=\frac{\omega_0}{2Q}\frac{\pi}{2\xi A^{3/2}}$, and
$\int d\omega\,S_{V,a}^2=\frac{\pi^2}4m^4\Phi_a^4\,Q_a/m$. With $R_0=\omega_0L/Q$:

$$\Theta(m)=\tau(m)\,\frac{\pi^2}8\,Q_a\,Q\,\bar G\,\frac{\Phi_a^4}{L_p^2}\ \Longrightarrow\
\frac{d\omega_0}{dt}\equiv\frac1\tau=\frac{\pi^2}{8}\frac{Q_a}{\Theta_{95}}\frac{\Phi_a^4}{L_p^2}\,Q\,\bar G(\omega_0)\quad[\mathrm{eV^2}] \tag{S5.1}$$

$$\text{SI:}\qquad \frac{d\omega_0}{dt}=\frac{\pi^2}{8}\frac{Q_a}{\Theta_{95}}\frac{\Phi_a[\mathrm{Wb}]^4}{\hbar^2L[\mathrm H]^2}\,Q\,\bar G\quad[\mathrm{rad/s^2}] \tag{S5.2}$$

Cross-checks against the literature (all agree identically):
(i) [C] F58 with $U_{\rm DM}=\Phi_a^2/4L$ and time per $\ln\nu$; (ii) [M3] Eq. 5 and [Core] Eq. 3 with
SNR² → Θ, $6.4\times10^5$ → $Q_a$, $c_{PU}^4B_0^4V^{10/3}=\Psi^4/4L_p^2$ (from [M3] A5), $d\nu=d\omega/2\pi$, and
$V$ = peak EMF ($m_a\Phi_a$); (iii) numerically, [GUT] Eq. 3 (41 kHz/yr) is reproduced as 41.28 kHz/yr;
(iv) the exact bin-by-bin sum (S5.9) of a discretised scan reproduces (S5.1) to ≤2×10⁻⁵ in $g$ (Sec. 8).

Assumptions: weak signal per bin; $t_i\gg\tau_{\rm coh}$ and $\gg$ ring-up $2Q/\omega_0$ (amplitude);
steps $\ll\Delta\omega_{2\times}$; $\Delta\omega_{2\times}\ll\omega_0$; $\tau$, $Q$, $T$, $\eta_A$, $\Psi$, $L_p$ slowly varying
over $\Delta\omega_{2\times}$; $\xi=\xi_{\rm opt}$ re-tuned per step; MQS.

### 5.2 Requirement $\mathcal F_{\rm req}$

Total time $t=\int d\omega_0/(d\omega_0/dt)$ at target coupling $g_t(\omega_0)$ gives, for fixed geometry,

$$\mathcal F\ge\mathcal F_{\rm req}=\frac{8\,\Theta_{95}}{\pi^2Q_a\,t}\int_{\omega_1}^{\omega_2}\frac{d\omega}{Q(\omega)\,\kappa_a(\omega)^4\,\bar G_{\rm opt}(\omega)}\quad[\mathrm{eV^{-2}}]. \tag{S5.3}$$

With $g_t\propto m_a$ ($\kappa_a=\kappa_1\omega/\omega_1$), constant $Q,T,\eta_A$ and (S3.11), the integrand is
$\propto\omega^{-5}$: $\int=\frac{6\sqrt3\eta_AT}{4\kappa_1^4}[1-\omega_1^4/\omega_2^4]$ and $\kappa_1^4=4g_1^4\rho^2$, so

$$\mathcal F_{\rm req}=\frac{3\sqrt3}{\pi^2}\,\frac{\Theta_{95}\,\eta_A\,T}{Q_a\,Q\,\rho_{\rm DM}^2\,g_{a\gamma\gamma}^4(m_1)\,t}\Big[1-\frac{\omega_1^4}{\omega_2^4}\Big] \tag{S5.4}$$

SI form (`F_req_closed_form_si`; equals $\mu_0^2\times$ natural to 1e-15):
$\mathcal F_{\rm req}^{\rm SI}=\frac{12\sqrt3}{\pi^2}\frac{\Theta_{95}\eta_Ak_BT\hbar}{Q_aQ(\kappa_1^{\rm SI})^4t}[1-\omega_1^4/\omega_2^4]$ with $\kappa_1^{\rm SI}$ in H$^{-1}$.
93.8% of the time is spent in the lowest octave. The built-in benchmarks (`optimistic`: Q = 2×10⁷, T = 10 mK,
η_A = 0.1; `conservative`: 10⁶, 30 mK, 10; `conservative_solenoid`: 10⁵, 30 mK, 10) are illustrative presets: a
study replaces them with the quality factor, temperature and amplifier noise of its own readout. Values (Sec. 8):
optimistic $1.04378\times10^4$ T⁴m¹⁰,
conservative toroid/CORE $6.26267\times10^7$, conservative solenoid $6.26267\times10^8$; exact $\bar G$ and $n$ change these by
$5.6\times10^{-8}$ and $9.3\times10^{-7}$.

### 5.3 Time allocation

*Target curve* (minimum total time to reach $g_t(m)$ everywhere; this is what (S5.3)–(S5.4) assume):

$$\tau(\omega_0)=\frac{\Theta_{95}}{\frac{\pi^2}{8}Q_aQ\bar G\,\kappa_a(g_t)^4\mathcal F},\qquad t_{\rm req}=\int\tau\,d\omega_0;\quad\text{rescaled to a live time }t:\ \tau\to\tau\,t/t_{\rm req}. \tag{S5.5–S5.6}$$

Optimality: in the local approximation $\Theta(m)=\tau(m)r(m)$, so $\Theta\ge\Theta_{95}\ \forall m$ is met with minimum
$\int\tau$ by saturation. For $\mathcal F=\mathcal F_{\rm req}$ the optimistic benchmark has
$\tau=498.6$ s/(rad/s) at 120.9 kHz and 0.01345 s/(rad/s) at 991 kHz ($\tau\propto\omega^{-5}$).

*Log-uniform* in mass and coupling ([C] Eqs. 227–245):

$$\tau(\omega_0)=\frac{t}{\omega_0\ln(\omega_2/\omega_1)}\quad(\text{constant time per }\ln\omega). \tag{S5.7}$$

More generally ([C] Eqs. 227–246), for weight $W(\nu,g)\propto W_\nu(\nu)/g$ (log-uniform in $g$, arbitrary in mass)
maximising $\int d\nu\,W_\nu\ln(1/g_{\min})$ under fixed $t$ gives $\tau(\nu)\propto W_\nu(\nu)$ (per unit $\nu$), independent
of the detector, because $g_{\min}^{-4}\propto\tau$. Any other prior: maximise [C] Eq. 227 numerically with (S5.8).
Note [C]'s $\tau$ is time per unit $\ln\nu$ ($=\omega\,\tau_\omega$).

### 5.4 Reach curve

Local:

$$g_{95}(m)=\Big[\frac{2\,\Theta_{95}}{\pi^2\,\tau(m)\,Q_a\,\rho_{\rm DM}^2\,\mathcal F\,Q\,\bar G_{\rm opt}(m)}\Big]^{1/4}\quad(\text{natural units}). \tag{S5.8}$$

Target allocation: $g_{95}=g_t\,(t_{\rm req}/t)^{1/4}=g_t(\mathcal F_{\rm req}/\mathcal F)^{1/4}$. Log-uniform with
$\bar G\propto\omega$: $g_{95}$ independent of $m$; for a 0.5–4.1 neV scan it is
$[4\ln(\omega_2/\omega_1)/(1-\omega_1^4/\omega_2^4)]^{1/4}=1.7034$ times the target-allocation reach at $m_1$.

Discrete/exact (to be used by the C++ code; replaces the local approximation):

$$\Theta(m,g)=\sum_i\frac{t_i}{2\pi}\int_0^{v_{\max}}m\,v\,dv\;h\!\big(s_i(m(1+v^2/2))\big) \tag{S5.9}$$

with $s_i$ from (S3.2), (S3.6) using the full $|Z|^2$, $n(\omega)$, $\omega^2$ backaction, per-tuning
$\xi_i$ (default $\xi_{\rm opt}(n(\omega_{0,i}))$, $M_i$ from (S3.5)) and $v_{\max}=v_{\rm obs}+7v_0$ (Gauss–Legendre, 256 nodes
suffice; compute $\omega-\omega_{0,i}=(m-\omega_{0,i})+mv^2/2$ to avoid cancellation; $n$ via `expm1`). Then

$$g_{95}:\ \Theta_{\rm lim}(m,g_{95})=\Theta_{95}\quad(\text{weak: }g_{95}=g_{\rm ref}(\Theta_{95}/\Theta_{\rm weak}(g_{\rm ref}))^{1/4};\ \text{exact: 1-D root find, monotone in }g). \tag{S5.10}$$

### 5.5 Discretisation, dwell, ring-up and coherence

Steps $\delta\omega_0=\Delta\omega_{2\times}/N_s$ ($N_s\gtrsim3$–10; [C] Eq. 238), dwell $t_i=\tau(\omega_{0,i})\delta\omega_0$,
number of tunings $N=\int N_s\,d\omega_0/\Delta\omega_{2\times}$; at high $T$, $\Delta\omega_{2\times}\simeq2\sqrt3k_BT/(\hbar\eta_AQ)$ is
constant so $N\simeq N_s(\omega_2-\omega_1)/\Delta\omega_{2\times}$. Optimistic benchmark at $\mathcal F=\mathcal F_{\rm req}$, $N_s=10$:
$N=24\,120$; dwell $1.13\times10^5$ s at 120.9 kHz but only 3.05 s at 991 kHz, below the ring-up
$2Q/\omega_0=6.4$ s and comparable to $\tau_{\rm coh}\approx0.7$–1.8 s. Require $t_i\gtrsim10\max(2Q/\omega_0,\tau_{\rm coh})$
([C] Eq. 225: $t^*=\max(10^6/\nu,Q/\nu)$); near the top of a $g\propto m$ scan the step must be coarsened (or the scan
becomes overhead-limited and over-reaches the target). Because time there is negligible, $\mathcal F_{\rm req}$ is unaffected.

**Weak-signal validity.** The per-bin $s$ at $g_{95}$ is set by the total data within the sensitivity band, not by
the step size: for toy A (Sec. 8) $s_{\max}=0.006$ at 0.5 neV but 0.40 at 4.1 neV (only ~24 s of data inside
$\Delta\omega_{\rm eq}$ there), where the exact Asimov (S4.3) gives $g_{95}$ 8.5% weaker than the weak form
(11% for toy B). Implementations should evaluate $h_{\rm lim}$ exactly and warn when $s_{\max}>0.05$.

## 6. Finite-band generalisation (broadband / copper / few settings)

When $\Delta\omega_{2\times}/\omega_0$ is not small (low-$Q$ normal-metal pickups, large $n/\eta_A$), when $R(\omega)$,
$n(\omega)$ or the $\omega^2$ backaction vary across the band, or when only discrete settings exist, use the
matched-filter coverage with the actual impedance. A *setting* $j$ is a tuning capacitance $C_j$ (i.e. $\omega_{0,j}$)
and a coupling $\xi_j$ (i.e. $M_j$, or a noise impedance); $\xi_j$ need not equal $\xi_{\rm opt}$.

$$K_j(m)=\frac1{2\pi}\int d\omega\,h\big(s_j(\omega)\big)\quad[\mathrm{s^{-1}}]\quad(\text{exact; Θ per unit dwell at coupling }g_t(m)), \tag{S6.1–S6.2}$$
$$K_j(m)\simeq\frac\pi8\,Q_a\,\frac{m^3\Phi_a^4}{N_{V,j}(m)^2}\quad(\text{narrow line: }N_{V,j}\text{ constant over }m/Q_a), \tag{S6.3}$$
$$\min_{t_j\ge0}\sum_jt_j\quad\text{s.t.}\quad\sum_jt_jK_j(m_k)\ge\Theta_{95}\ \ \forall k\quad(\text{LP; verify on a denser }m\text{ grid}). \tag{S6.4}$$

**Implementation.** `kernel_row`, `kernel_narrow_line` and `lp_schedule` take the single-pole
`Detector`; `kernel_row_noise`, `kernel_narrow_line_noise`, `g95_band` and `reach_band` take an
externally supplied $N_V(\omega)$ per setting, which is how the *exact* noise of a readout
network (`readout_circuit.md` (C4.5)) enters — all that (S6.1) needs from the readout is
$N_V(\omega)$ and the pickup's $\Psi$. `axionfem.scan.finite_band` drives the whole chain from a
`circuit.Readout`; `docs/validation/finite_band.md` reproduces the demonstration below from the
exact circuit (local time to 7e-5, LP time to 6e-4, LP/local 1.1727 vs 1.1735) and measures where
the single-pole reduction fails.

In DMRadio-style variables (S6.3) reads $1.6\times10^5|V_{\rm peak}|^4/(\mathrm{SNR}^2fN^{(1)2})$ with
$Q_a=6.4\times10^5$, $\Theta=\mathrm{SNR}^2$, $N^{(1)}=2N_V$, $f=m/2\pi$; an amplifier model
$S^{(1)}_{II}=\eta hf/Z_N$, $S^{(1)}_{VV}=\eta hfZ_N$ is (S3.5) with $Z_N=\eta_AR_0/\xi$.
Demonstration (reference code; $\Psi=100$ T m³, $L_p=1$ m, $Q=10^4$, 30 mK, $\eta_A=10$, 120.9–400 kHz;
$\Delta\omega_{2\times}/\omega_0=0.18$ at the bottom): the local schedule evaluated with (S6.1) covers the interior at
1.05–1.13 × Θ₉₅ (convexity of $\tau\propto\omega^{-5}$ across the wide band) but only 0.37 at the band edges; the LP
optimum needs 1.173 × the local time; (S6.3) agrees with (S6.1) to 6.5×10⁻⁵.

## 7. Long-detector limits

## 9. Conventions and notes on the literature

1. **Consistency.** The scan rate (S5.1), $\bar G$/$\xi_{\rm opt}$ (S3.8–S3.9), the $\eta_A$ and $\xi$ definitions with
   the Foster normalisation, $\kappa_a=g\sqrt{2\rho}$ as an *amplitude*, and $\mathcal F_{\rm req}$ (S5.4) are mutually
   consistent and agree with [C], [M3], [Core] and [GUT] after the mappings of Sec. 5.1 (analytic, plus the numerical
   cross-checks of Sec. 8).
2. **$Q_a$ rounding.** The SHM value is 6.7795×10⁵; the default `Q_axion` = 6.8×10⁵ is that value rounded (+0.30%).
   With the exact value $\mathcal F_{\rm req}=1.0469\times10^4$ instead of $1.0438\times10^4$ (0.30% in Θ, 0.076% in
   $g$). A calculation that integrates the real line differs from the local formulas with the default by exactly
   this; compare with the same $Q_a$ on both sides.
3. **DMRadio's 6.4×10⁵** ([M3] Eq. 5, [GUT], [Core]) corresponds to $v_{\rm obs}\approx248.6$ km/s with $v_0=220$ (e.g. near
   the June maximum); together with their ρ=0.45 and SNR=3 (Θ=9) this sets their normalisation. Inferred, not stated by them.
4. **[C] Eq. B9 ($4.4\times10^5$) is not reproduced.** [C] B1 writes $\sigma_v=\sqrt{3/2}\,v_c$ inside
   $e^{-v^2/2\sigma_v^2}$, which taken literally gives $3.1\times10^5$; the standard SHM gives 6.7×10⁵.
5. **[F] LEE examples use two-sided p.** [F] text (Eqs. 58–60, and its N=1 example TS=9 at 3σ) is one-sided, but its quoted
   40.9/57.5 (3σ/5σ, $N_{m_a}\sim3\times10^7$) match two-sided p (40.83/57.41) vs one-sided 42.18/58.77. The default here is
   one-sided.
6. **Θ₉₅ = 2.71** is the rounded $2.7055$ (effect −0.16% on $\mathcal F_{\rm req}$); kept as 2.71 by convention.
7. **Scan endpoint:** 4.1 neV is 991.4 kHz; the default scan band (`scan.f_max`) ends at 1 MHz. The effect on
   $\mathcal F_{\rm req}$ is $7.5\times10^{-6}$.
8. **Sensitivity bandwidth** has inequivalent definitions (S3.12) differing by $\sqrt3$. The scan rate does not depend
   on the choice.
9. **Regime of validity at the top of the scan** (Sec. 5.5): dwell per tuning approaches the ring-up time and per-bin
   $s$ reaches 0.4; exact-Asimov reach is up to ~9–11% weaker than (S5.8) near 4 neV for the toy detectors. Does not
   affect $\mathcal F_{\rm req}$ (93.8% of the time in the first octave).
10. **[M3] "$V_{pp}$"** is the peak amplitude $m_a\Phi_a$ (by its Eq. A5), not peak-to-peak.
11. **Units in SI:** $\xi$ and $\eta_A$ require explicit $\hbar$ ($\xi=2R S_\Phi/\hbar\omega_0M^2$, $\eta_A=2\sqrt{S_\Phi S_J}/\hbar$).

## 10. Implementation notes

* The C++ is **SI internally** and also reports natural-HL values (fields in T, lengths in m).
  The Python reference is natural-eV internally; the two must agree at the interface. SI recipe (all $\hbar$, $k_B$ explicit):
  $\Psi_{\rm SI}=\mu_0\Psi$, $L=\mu_0L_p$ (S1.3); $\Phi_a[\mathrm{Wb}]=\kappa_a^{\rm SI}\Psi_{\rm SI}$ with
  $\kappa_a^{\rm SI}=g_{\rm SI}\sqrt{2\rho_{\rm SI}\hbar c}/\mu_0$ (S2.3); $\omega_a=m_ac^2/\hbar$, $\mathcal L(\omega)$ in s;
  $S_{V,a}=\frac\pi2\Phi_a^2\omega^4\omega_a^{-2}\mathcal L$ [V² s]; $S_{V,\rm th}=\hbar\omega R(2n+1)$, $n=1/\mathrm{expm1}(\hbar\omega/k_BT)$;
  $S_\Phi/M^2=\hbar\xi\omega_0/2R_0$, $\omega^2M^2S_J=\hbar\eta_A^2R_0\omega^2/(2\xi\omega_0)$ (S3.5); $s=S_{V,a}/N_V$ (S3.6);
  rate (S5.2); requirement: SI form under (S5.4). Natural-unit values are $\Psi_{\rm SI}/\mu_0$ [T m³], $L/\mu_0$ [m],
  $\mathcal F_{\rm SI}/\mu_0^2$ [T⁴ m¹⁰].
* $n(\omega,T)$ via `expm1`; never form $\omega$ and $\omega_0$ separately when differencing (use $(m-\omega_0)+mv^2/2$).
* Integrate the line in $v$ (Gauss–Legendre on $[0,v_{\rm obs}+7v_0]$); the integrand $\propto f^2/v$ is smooth.
* For a dense scan only tunings within ±40 noise-doubling half-widths matter (truncation error <10⁻⁵).
* LP (S6.4): rescale the kernel to O(1) before calling the solver (HiGHS tolerances).
* $Q_a$, Θ₉₅, $C_\gamma$, $m_af_a$, $\rho$, $v_0$, $v_{\rm obs}$ and the sidedness are inputs; the defaults are those of
  Sec. 8 (the design's `scan` section, `ScanParams` in C++).
